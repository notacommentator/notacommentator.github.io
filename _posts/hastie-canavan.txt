Why is Hastie not leader?
- He stepped back during the first spill because he knew his support would get split with Taylor's and he'd likely lose just because of that
- When Ley won, but then began to falter, he stepped aside to let Angus take the leadership
- - Becoming leader at the start, the libs were not in an unsalvageable position and he could have perhaps turned the ship around
- - But by the time of the second spill, things had gone from terrible to abysmal
- - There were three possibilities if he let Angus lead:
1. Angus fails miserably and gets booted out. In that case Hastie becomes leader.
2. Angus struggles, but survives until the election, which he loses. In that case, Hastie can try to win again at the next election. Hastie's seat of canning is one of the few seats where the Liberals had a swing towards them, and being a more high-profile candidate makes it likely he will retain it next election.
3. Angus does well and wins the election. In that case, Hastie is positioned to act as the natural successor.
The point is, Hastie is young. He has plenty of time to become leader and he knows it. Taylor does not and he probably thinks it's 'his turn' to lead. If Hastie had contested the leadership, there was no way Taylor would have simply stepped aside. And if he had won, it'd be likely that the pro-Angus faction of the party would have undermined him.
The point is, Hastie is now placed to be the next leader of the party. Probably the only thing that could get in his way is if Josh Frydenberg decides to contest his seat at the next election and wins. There aren't many other good leadership options left: the Shadow Cabinet is pretty bare.
